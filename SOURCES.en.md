# Sources

## The manuscript

Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library, Yale University, **GEN MSS VOL 855**,
"Notebook of veterinary recipes : manuscript", 1710–1716.

- Catalogue: https://collections.library.yale.edu/catalog/33569870
- IIIF manifest: https://collections.library.yale.edu/manifests/33569870
- 76 images, all digitised and public.

The owner signs himself **Thomas Fossey** (image 5, "Thomas Fossey 1716"). Several recipes end
"Approved by me Tho: Fossey" or "Ed. Fossey approved". The endpapers also carry the names
William Edward and William Feniel.

Images are cited by the number in the IIIF manifest (1–76), because the notebook's own pagination
covers only part of it.

Cipher digits occur on these 38 images:
6, 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 36, 40, 41, 43, 44, 45, 47, 49, 51, 53, 54, 55,
56, 57, 59, 60, 61, 62, 63, 64, 65, 66, 67, 69, 70.
Of these, 29 and 30 photograph the same opening as 25–34 with the pasted slips in another position;
they were checked and show no cipher line that is not visible elsewhere.

No images are kept here. Anyone can fetch them from the catalogue above:

## The catalogue item this work answers

Item **818** of Daniel Bourdeau's catalogue of unsolved ciphers,
"Thomas Fossey's notebook of veterinary recipes, sections in a vowel-to-digit cipher".
https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver — see `docs/PRIOR_CHECK.en.md` for what was already known.

## The vocabulary used to judge a reading

Three sources: an English dictionary (macOS /usr/share/dict/words, the headwords of Webster's
Second International); the spellings that occur in the notebook's own plain-text pages
(`data/plain_vocab.txt`); and period names of drugs and measures (`data/materia.txt`,
each entry saying on which image the letters were checked, or that it is a standard name of the
period's veterinary and pharmacy books).
